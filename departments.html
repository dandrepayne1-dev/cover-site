Ø1 // The Exposure

Compliance can tell them the rules. Nobody is teaching them the money.

NIL and endorsement income can arrive as 1099 income with no withholding and little structure around the money. Athletes sign deals at eighteen and meet the tax bill at twenty. The department sees the consequences in the spring, in the portal, and in the parent phone call.

Your staff has a real role in educating athletes. But individualized financial guidance requires a different kind of relationship. CØVER™ gives the athlete that dedicated coaching relationship without putting the department in the middle of it.

Ø2 // The Firewall Clause

The school pays. The athlete is the client.

This is the structural core of the seat license, written into the contract rather than promised in a meeting. The department funds access. The engagement itself runs between CØVER™ and the athlete.

Data Access // Department

What the department receives

  • Seats issued and seats claimed
  • Workshop attendance by session
  • Program completion rates, roster level
  • Aggregate, anonymized engagement summary
  • Quarterly program review for leadership

What the department never sees

  • Any athlete's income or deal values
  • Bank balances or account activity
  • Tax positions or filing status
  • Individual reports or coaching notes
  • Anything tied to a named athlete

Athletes are told this in plain language at onboarding. It is the reason they participate honestly, and the reason the program produces results your staff can point to.

The department provides the access. CØVER™ provides the financial system. The athlete owns the relationship.

Ø3 // The Long Game

Not a four-year program. Financial development that follows the athlete.

Most financial education for athletes is one session in the fall that nobody remembers by spring. CØVER™ runs in stages tied to where an athlete actually is, not to eligibility year. An athlete might transfer, sign a new deal, go pro, or graduate early. The system develops with them through all of it.

Start

What income actually means, what has to be set aside, and how to build structure around the first checks.

Build

Income, expenses, records, savings, debt, and taxes stop living in different places and start living in one system.

Grow

Decisions get bigger than the next check. The athlete has the information in front of them before they decide.

Transition

Transfer, graduation, a professional career, a new contract. The school can change. The system does not have to.

Legacy

Habits, records, and knowledge that continue long after the athletic career changes.

The department funds the start. The athlete keeps the system.

The Baseline Assessment places each athlete at the right stage on day one, so transfers and upperclassmen are never behind. Seats are annual and campus funded. What continues after an athlete leaves your program is the athlete's own relationship with CØVER™, at their election.
